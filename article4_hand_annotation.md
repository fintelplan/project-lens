**"Before Washington sharply cut foreign help in early 2025, South Africa relied on the US for nearly 17 per cent of its HIV budget, or more than US$400 million annually, according to the South African National Aids Council."**

> 【 Strong factual paragraphs. The contrast is built honestly:
> - China's grant: $3.49 million for 54,000 students/drug users

> Two readings:

The structural arithmetic that would expose the headline's "huge gap remains" claim is buried in numbers reader must compute.

> "China's first major entry" — narrator-voice claim about strategic significance. **OP-022 candidate (light)** — strategic-attribution to "China" treats Xi Office's ad-hoc grants as if they constitute "entry" into a sector. This is editorial framing. 】

**"When the Donald Trump administration dismantled the United States Agency for International Development (USAID), cancelling most grants and leading to a sharp drop in health aid to Africa, some nations lost more than half their funding."**

> 【 Important framing moment. "When the Donald Trump administration dismantled USAID..." — Trump administration named specifically (good per PHI-003). The verb "dismantled" is accurate (USAID restructuring under second-term Trump is documented).
> 
> "Some nations lost more than half their funding" — narrator-voice claim, no source. **OP-022 candidate (moderate)** — strategic claim about scale of impact stated as fact without sourcing.

> **OP-001 candidate WEAK** — the article frames the question as "did China step up to fill the void Trump created?" — when alternative framing could be "is great-power aid competition serving African peoples?" The question framed pre-narrows the analytical scope.
> 
> But this is genuinely borderline. The article IS about Beijing's aid stepup. The frame is appropriate to the subject. NOT a clear OP-001 case. Light at most. 】
